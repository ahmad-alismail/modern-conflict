# MODERN CONFLICT
## Game Vision & Core Requirements

**Genre:** Modern Military Real-Time Strategy (RTS) / Tactical Warfare Simulation  
**Platform:** PC  
**Perspective:** 3D strategic view with cinematic zoom  
**Gameplay:** Real-time with tactical pause  
**Setting:** Modern fictional conflicts in realistic environments

---

## 1. Game Vision

Modern Conflict is an immersive, visually realistic modern warfare strategy game inspired by *Command & Conquer: Generals*, with a stronger emphasis on tactical decision-making, modern military equipment, and believable battlefield behavior.

Players command military forces including infantry, armored vehicles, tanks, helicopters, aircraft, drones, and support units across detailed urban and open environments.

The goal is to create an engaging and authentic-feeling military experience where different units have meaningful strengths, weaknesses, and limitations.

Unlike traditional base-building RTS games, Modern Conflict focuses on **commanding forces and experiencing battles rather than managing factories, economies, or resource production**.

The game should be enjoyable as a standalone offline experience against computer-controlled opponents, while also supporting optional AI-powered commanders in the future.

## 2. Core Gameplay

### Real-Time with Tactical Pause

The simulation runs continuously, but players can pause the action at any time to inspect the battlefield, select units, and issue or modify commands.

Commands issued during tactical pause are executed when the simulation resumes.

Players can switch smoothly between a strategic overview and a close-up cinematic perspective.

### Tactical Decision-Making

Gameplay should emphasize:

- Commanding individual units and groups.
- Responding to changing battlefield conditions.
- Managing incomplete information and visibility.
- Understanding the strengths and weaknesses of different units.
- Making meaningful decisions under uncertainty.
- Coordinating different types of military equipment.
- Achieving mission objectives while minimizing unnecessary losses.

Combat should feel believable and consequential, with gameplay mechanics that represent modern military capabilities without requiring exact real-world weapon or operational simulations.

### Simplified Reinforcements

There is no factory construction, resource collection, economic development, or technology research.

Each scenario begins with predefined forces, with optional limited reinforcements depending on the mission.

The player's attention remains on battlefield command.

## 3. Military Factions

The game features asymmetric military factions with different technologies, capabilities, equipment, and limitations.

### Light Forces

A lightly equipped force primarily consisting of infantry, support units, and light vehicles, with limited access to advanced technology.

### Conventional Military

A modern organized military equipped with infantry, armored vehicles, tanks, helicopters, aircraft, and supporting systems.

### Advanced Autonomous Forces

A technologically advanced force specializing in drones, unmanned ground vehicles, robotics, observation systems, and electronic technologies.

Factions should not simply have different appearances. Their available equipment and capabilities should create distinct gameplay experiences.

The game should reflect meaningful differences between technologically advanced and less-equipped forces while maintaining engaging scenarios for both sides.

## 4. Realistic Combat Experience

The combat system should provide an immersive representation of modern warfare.

Important elements include:

- Distinct movement and behavior for infantry, vehicles, and aircraft.
- Meaningful differences in unit protection, mobility, observation, and effectiveness.
- Terrain, obstacles, and buildings influencing gameplay.
- Fog of war and limited battlefield information.
- Unit morale and damage states.
- Simplified ammunition and equipment limitations.
- Environmental effects such as smoke, dust, weather, and reduced visibility.
- Visible equipment damage and persistent battlefield consequences.
- Responsive and believable computer-controlled unit behavior.

The goal is not to maximize complexity but to create a **convincing, understandable, and enjoyable tactical experience**.

## 5. Immersive 3D Environments

Visual quality is a major priority.

The game should feature detailed, realistic-looking environments with convincing buildings, roads, terrain, lighting, weather, animations, and environmental effects.

Maps may be inspired by real-world cities, particularly Syrian and Middle Eastern urban architecture, while representing fictionalized environments rather than exact operational conflict locations.

The player should be able to zoom out for strategic command and zoom in to observe units and the environment in detail.

The world should respond visually to events through movement, damage, smoke, debris, and environmental changes.

**The intended experience is to feel like observing and commanding a living battlefield, rather than moving abstract pieces on a map.**

## 6. Custom Map Creation

A major feature is the ability to create playable environments from uploaded area plans.

Players should eventually be able to:

1. Upload an image, drawing, or plan of an area.
2. Have the game interpret its general layout, including roads, buildings, and open spaces.
3. Generate a realistic-looking, fictionalized 3D map based on that layout.
4. Review and edit the resulting environment.
5. Save the map and use it in gameplay.

The map editor should also support creating environments manually.

Generated maps should be editable rather than fixed, and uncertain details should be customizable.

The goal is to make custom map creation accessible without requiring professional 3D modeling skills.

## 7. Computer Opponents and AI Modes

The game supports two independent opponent systems.

### Classic Computer AI — Default

The game must be fully playable offline against conventional computer-controlled opponents, without LLMs, API keys, subscriptions, or token costs.

Three difficulty levels:

- **Easy:** Beginner-friendly opponents with simpler decision-making.
- **Medium:** Balanced and competent opponents.
- **Hard:** More challenging opponents with improved decisions and adaptability.

Difficulty should primarily affect decision-making quality, not provide unfair information or artificial equipment advantages.

### LLM Commander — Optional

A separate, optional mode allows language-model agents to command factions.

This may support:

- Locally running language models.
- Cloud-based models such as Claude or GPT.
- AI-versus-AI matches.
- Spectator mode to observe autonomous commanders and their decisions.

LLM integration must never be required for normal gameplay, and paid AI services must never be activated automatically.

## 8. Game Modes

**Skirmish:** Play against classic computer AI with selectable difficulty.

**Scenario:** Complete fictional missions with predefined objectives and forces.

**Sandbox:** Freely place units, configure conditions, and experiment with the simulation.

**Custom Map:** Create or import a map and play on it.

**AI vs AI:** Watch autonomous commanders compete, using classic AI or optional LLM-powered agents.

**Multiplayer (Future):** Play against other human players.

## 9. User Experience

The game should offer intuitive controls familiar to traditional RTS players.

Essential capabilities include:

- Selecting and commanding units individually or in groups.
- Issuing and queuing orders.
- Tactical pause and simulation speed controls.
- Smooth camera movement, zoom, and rotation.
- Minimap and fog of war.
- Clear unit status and mission information.
- Responsive controls and readable visual feedback.
- Saving, loading, and replaying scenarios.
- Configurable graphical and gameplay settings.

The interface should remain clean and intuitive despite the depth of the simulation.

## 10. Initial Playable Experience

The first playable version should demonstrate the game's identity through:

- One visually convincing fictional urban map.
- Real-time gameplay with tactical pause.
- Smooth strategic and cinematic camera controls.
- Two distinct military factions.
- Infantry and several vehicle classes.
- Unit selection, movement, and combat.
- Basic damage, visibility, and environmental effects.
- A functional offline computer opponent.
- Easy, Medium, and Hard difficulty.
- Clear mission objectives and victory/defeat conditions.

Advanced map generation, additional equipment, deeper simulation, and LLM commanders can expand the experience later.

## 11. What the Game Is Not

Modern Conflict is not intended to be:

- A factory-building or resource-mining game.
- An economic or city-management simulator.
- A technology research-tree game.
- A turn-based strategy game.
- A game dependent on paid AI APIs.
- A simulation requiring players to learn real-world military doctrine.
- An exact reconstruction of real-world military operations.

The focus is on **immersive tactical gameplay, realistic-looking environments, distinctive military capabilities, and intelligent opponents**.

## 12. Ultimate Goal

Create a modern warfare RTS that combines:

- The accessibility of *Command & Conquer: Generals*.
- The atmosphere and visual quality of modern military games.
- The tactical depth of a believable battlefield simulation.
- Distinct strengths and weaknesses across military factions.
- Engaging, completely offline computer opponents.
- Real-time gameplay with tactical pause.
- Player-created 3D environments from uploaded area plans.
- Optional autonomous AI commanders.

**The ultimate experience:** A player enters a detailed modern urban environment, commands military forces with distinct capabilities, observes a dynamic and believable battlefield, makes meaningful tactical decisions, and competes against intelligent opponents—all without needing to manage an economy or pay for AI tokens.
